# Knowledge Distillation

**Category:** AI Implementation / Enterprise AI  
**Related:** [[AI Operating Layer]], [[Enterprise AI Adoption]], Tacit Knowledge  

## Overview

Knowledge Distillation is the systematic conversion of expert judgment and operational decisions into machine-readable training signals. This process is critical for organizations seeking to build defensible AI systems based on proprietary expertise.

## The Problem

**Tacit Knowledge Challenge:**
- Expertise in services organizations is tacit and perishable
- Best operators know things they cannot easily articulate:
  - Heuristics developed over years
  - Edge-case intuitions
  - Pattern recognition below conscious reasoning
- Knowledge leaves when experts leave

**Traditional Approach:**
- Documentation (incomplete, quickly outdated)
- Training programs (time-intensive, variable quality)
- Apprenticeship models (doesn't scale)

## The Solution

**Knowledge Distillation Process:**
1. Capture expert decisions in operational context
2. Convert judgments into machine-readable signals
3. Use signals to train AI systems
4. Feed improvements back into operations
5. System continuously improves

## Strategic Value

**Creates Defensible Advantage:**
- Proprietary training data from operations
- Can't be replicated by competitors
- Improves with scale (more operations = more signals)
- Compounds over time

**Three Compounding Assets:**
1. Proprietary operational data
2. Domain expert workforce generating training signals
3. Accumulated tacit knowledge about complex work

## Implementation Requirements

**Technical:**
- Instrumentation to capture decisions
- Signal processing pipelines
- ML training infrastructure
- Feedback loop mechanisms

**Organizational:**
- Expert participation in distillation process
- Culture of continuous improvement
- Governance for quality control
- Change management for AI integration

## Use Cases

**Services Organizations:**
- Healthcare diagnosis and treatment decisions
- Legal case strategy and document review
- Financial advisory recommendations
- Customer support resolution patterns

**Enterprise Operations:**
- IT incident response decisions
- Supply chain optimization choices
- Quality control judgments
- Risk assessment evaluations
